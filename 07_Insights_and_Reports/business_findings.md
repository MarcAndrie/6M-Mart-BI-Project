# 6M Mart — Business Findings

**Project:** Retail Intelligence & Performance Analytics  
**Business:** 6M Mart  
**Analysis Period:** January 1, 2024 – December 31, 2025  
**Currency:** Philippine Peso (PHP)  
**Data Classification:** Synthetic / Simulated Data

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The analysis examined sales performance, branch performance, product and category profitability, promotional effectiveness, inventory availability, returns, and business opportunities across eight 6M Mart branches.

Key findings:

- Total net sales reached **₱37.52 million**, generating **₱10.59 million** in gross profit at a **28.21% gross profit margin**.
- Overall sales remained nearly flat between 2024 and 2025, increasing by only **0.23%**.
- Las Piñas recorded the strongest branch sales growth at **13.72%**, while Bacoor experienced the largest decline at **10.17%**.
- Groceries contributed the largest share of revenue at **41.95%**, while Personal Care recorded the highest category gross profit margin.
- Promoted sales had a lower gross profit margin than non-promoted sales: **21.53% versus 29.63%**.
- Bacoor had a notably higher inventory stockout rate than the other branches.
- Overall return performance remained relatively stable, with the annual return rate increasing slightly from **1.53% to 1.54%**.

These findings identify opportunities to improve branch performance, promotional planning, product profitability, and inventory management.

## 6. Promotional Effectiveness

| Metric | Non-Promoted | Promoted |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net Sales | ₱30.97M | ₱6.56M |
| Gross Profit | ₱9.17M | ₱1.41M |
| Gross Profit Margin | 29.63% | 21.53% |

Promoted sales had an **8.10 percentage-point lower gross profit margin** than non-promoted sales.

Additional findings:

- PROM011 (Summer Sale) generated the highest promotion-level net sales and gross profit.
- PROM005 (Back to School Deals) recorded the highest promotion-level margin at 24.86%.
- PROM004 (Mid-Year Mega Sale) recorded the lowest margin at 16.98%.
- Promoted gross profit margins were lower across all six product categories.
- Fixed-amount promotion records had a higher margin than percentage-based promotion records, but only one fixed-amount campaign was available for comparison.

Promotions generated substantial sales activity, but their lower margins suggest that campaign profitability should be considered alongside revenue and volume.

**Limitation:** These results show an association between promotion participation and lower margins, not proof that promotions caused the margin difference. Transactions can also contain both promoted and non-promoted product lines, so their order counts overlap.

## 7. Inventory Analysis

### Branch availability

Bacoor recorded **320 stockout snapshots**, substantially higher than the other branches, which recorded only zero to two each.

Bacoor also had the highest low-stock rate at 21.89%.

Las Piñas had the highest overstock rate at 26.01%, while Bacoor had the lowest at 18.78%.

### Product-level observations

Products with frequent stockouts in Bacoor included:

- Bell Pepper 500g — 48 stockout snapshots.
- Laundry Bar Soap — 44.
- Dishwashing Liquid 500ml — 44.
- Cooking Oil 1L — 40.
- Greek Yogurt 400g — 39.
- Chocolate Drink 250ml — 38.
- Chocolate Powder 300g — 33.
- Deodorant Roll-On — 32.

Eight products accounted for 318 of Bacoor's 320 stockout snapshots.

Inventory conditions also revealed potential overstocking elsewhere. For example, Cooking Oil 1L had stockout records in Bacoor while showing overstock records in several other branches.

Other products, including Aluminum Foil 10m and Cola 1.5L, exhibited persistently high low-stock rates across branches.

**Potential opportunity:** Review replenishment quantities and possible inventory redistribution between branches.

**Limitations:** Inventory data consists of weekly snapshots. It cannot establish the exact duration of stockouts or quantify lost sales. The overstock threshold of three times the reorder level is a project-defined analytical rule, not a universal inventory standard.

## 8. Returns and Refunds Analysis

### Branch and category observations

- Silang Household Essentials recorded the highest branch-category return rate at 2.13%.
- General Trias Personal Care followed at 2.12%.
- Groceries generated the largest refund amounts across several branches, reflecting their larger sales volume.

### Monthly return trends

- Highest monthly cohort return rate: February 2024 at 1.94%.
- Lowest monthly cohort return rate: November 2024 at 1.25%.
- Highest returned units in a month: December 2024, with 210 units.
- Highest monthly refunds: December 2024, at ₱32,951.59.

### Annual return performance

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Returned Units | 1,901 | 1,917 |
| Return Records | 1,224 | 1,245 |
| Total Refunds | ₱289,717.35 | ₱298,486.31 |
| Return Rate | 1.53% | 1.54% |
| Average Refund per Record | ₱236.70 | ₱239.75 |

Return activity remained relatively stable between the two years. Refund amounts increased by 3.03%, while the return rate increased by only 0.01 percentage points.

### Products with higher return rates

Among products with at least 100 original units sold:

- Conditioner 340ml had the highest return rate at 3.05%.
- Evaporated Milk 370ml followed at 2.99%.
- Milk Tea Drink 500ml recorded 2.90%.
- Canned Sardines had the largest refund exposure among the top twenty products, at ₱7,325.24.

Return reasons were not available, so the analysis cannot establish why these products were returned.

**Methodology note:** Return rates are calculated by attributing returned units to the original sale period through the sales-line relationship. Late-2025 sales may have had less time to generate returns before the dataset cutoff.

## 9. Business Trends and Opportunities

### Branch opportunities

- **Bacoor:** Investigate declining transaction volume and recurring stockouts.
- **Las Piñas:** Study practices associated with strong sales growth while addressing declining average order value and elevated overstock.
- **Silang:** Investigate declining order count and average order value.
- **General Trias:** Examine the decline in order volume despite higher average order value.

### Category opportunities

- **Personal Care:** Explore opportunities to build on its relatively high margin and positive growth.
- **Household Essentials:** Review products with improving profitability and identify opportunities to increase revenue.
- **Groceries:** Investigate declining gross profit despite slight sales growth.
- **Dairy and Fresh Produce:** Examine the causes of declining sales and profitability.

### Product opportunities

- Review high-revenue products with declining gross profit, especially Chocolate Powder 300g, Soy Sauce 500ml, and White Sugar 1kg.
- Review high-revenue, low-margin products before making pricing or assortment decisions.
- Monitor growing products with inventory constraints, including Laundry Bar Soap, Cooking Oil 1L, and Deodorant Roll-On.
- Review persistent overstock in Las Piñas and possible stock redistribution opportunities.

### Promotional opportunities

Evaluate campaigns using gross profit and gross profit margin in addition to sales revenue. Future promotion comparisons should consider campaign duration, product mix, discount structure, and the number of campaigns in each group.

## 10. Methodology and Limitations

1. The dataset is synthetic and does not represent actual 6M Mart business records.
2. The analysis covers January 1, 2024 through December 31, 2025.
3. Net sales are calculated as gross sales less discounts and refunds.
4. Gross profit is calculated as net sales less cost of goods sold.
5. Returned quantities are deducted from original sold quantities when calculating net units and associated cost of goods sold.
6. Return rates use returned units divided by original units sold.
7. Returns are attributed to the original sale period for monthly and annual cohort analysis.
8. Inventory availability is based on weekly snapshots rather than continuous stock monitoring.
9. Overstock is defined for this project as inventory at least three times the reorder level.
10. Promotional comparisons are descriptive and do not establish causation.
11. Gross profit does not account for operating expenses and is not equivalent to net income.
12. Return reasons, lost sales, operating costs, and customer-level behavior were not available in the dataset.
